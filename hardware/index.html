The Starter writes sticks and Pi cards and holds no keys. Faraday Demo runs on a computer that is on a network. It is a simulator with Practice's samples, not a signing device.

How each is kept off the network

The stick and the Pi

The network stack is not compiled in: no Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or USB network driver, and no socket layer. A Pi 3's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip is never given its firmware, so it never runs. A PC's own disks are invisible.

Faraday Demo and the Starter

Faraday Demo and the Starter run on a computer that may be on a network. Neither is a place for real keys.

A 7-inch or 10-inch panel starts in landscape, and Settings › Display › Rotation turns it and its touch. A screen of 11 inches or less takes its density from its EDID. The app lays out every one of these sizes. The Pi images are built for the 2.8-inch panel and for an HDMI screen: faraday/image/panels/ holds those two and the PC's firmware framebuffer. A DPI or DSI screen needs its profile in the image: one image per screen and build, as docs/IMAGES.md specifies; until the 7-inch DSI's is built, a 7-inch screen is the stick's, on a small PC or tablet, or an HDMI one on the Pi.

Orientation

The layout follows the screen's width in each orientation: a 7-inch panel is expanded and low in landscape, with the rail, and compact in portrait, with the navigation bar at the foot. The Pi's 2.8-inch panel stays in portrait: it has no Rotation.

Motion

Every platform starts with full motion. Reduce motion (Settings › Appearance) is off on a first start. Where frames are slow, the automatic gate turns fades off by itself: it does so when the steps are more than 55 ms apart, and brings them back after 24 fast frames in a row.

Strict and Compatible

Each platform has two builds. A download page offers Strict where it is built, and the other build beside it.

PlatformStrictCompatible
The PiToday's kernel: no DRM, no HID, no USB storage; the DPI panel's touch is the only inputDRM for HDMI and the DSI panels; USB keyboards, mice and touchscreens; USB storage
The PC stickToday's kernel with efi=disable_early_pci_dma on its built-in command lineToday's kernel as it is

Strict is the most locked-down build a piece of hardware allows. Compatible works with more hardware. Some older PCs do not start with efi=disable_early_pci_dma. They take Compatible.

Who needs Compatible

  • A PC that does not start with Strict.
  • A Pi with a USB keyboard, a mouse or a USB stick.
  • A Pi with a DSI screen (5-inch, 7-inch, 10-inch) or an HDMI screen. These have no Strict image: the Strict Pi kernel has no DRM, and they need it.

What the Pi's Compatible adds

  • DRM for the display only: no GPU render node, no HDMI-CEC, no HDMI audio.
  • HDMI: the kernel reads a monitor's EDID.
  • USB keyboards, mice and touchscreens, under the PC stick's approval rules.
  • The screen's own USB touchscreen, named in the screen's profile, as trusted input.
  • USB storage, one partition at a time, read only while no secret is open, as on the PC.

Every build offered is safe in practice

Strict is the most each piece of hardware allows. Compatible adds what more hardware needs: the root filesystem, the app and its checks are the same in both builds of a platform, and the kernel and its command line differ.

Each build has its own list of forbidden kernel options, and the build fails if one is on. The Strict Pi list keeps DRM, HID and USB storage out, so a Strict image carries none of what Compatible adds.

The boards compared

What starts before Faraday, and where it is kept, differs from board to board.

Raspberry Pi 3 and Zero 2 W

Everything they start from is on the card: the firmware, and the kernel with the system linked into it. A fresh card is a clean start.

The chip's one-time-programmable memory is out of the app's reach: its mailbox driver is not built. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip is never given its firmware, so it never runs.

One card starts the 3B, 3B+ and Zero 2 W: it carries each board's device tree, and the firmware loads the one for the board it starts on. Nobody has started a Zero 2 W with it yet. The Zero 2 W has no DSI connector, so the DSI screens do not fit it.

Raspberry Pi 4 Planned

The Pi 4 starts from a boot loader kept in an EEPROM on the board, not on the card. A card carrying recovery.bin can rewrite it, so on a Pi 4 a fresh card is not a clean start. Its security is close to a PC's: its boot code is on the board. Its USB sits behind PCIe.

It will run the same images as the Pi 3: its 32-bit kernel, firmware and device tree beside the Pi 3's, the system the same. No Faraday card carries a boot loader update, and its kernel leaves out SPI_SPIDEV and SPI_BCM2835.

Before first use, restore Raspberry Pi's boot loader with Raspberry Pi Imager. A wire to test point TP5 locks the boot loader in hardware.

Whether the 2.8-inch screen runs on the Pi 4's firmware display path without DRM, as Strict needs, is to be tried on the board.

A laptop or PC

Its firmware runs before Faraday, and Faraday does not defend against compromised firmware. Nor against the management processors below the operating system, Intel's Management Engine and AMD's Platform Security Processor: What else runs on this computer says which computers have them and what to do.

Between the firmware and the kernel's IOMMU there is a window in which a PCI device the firmware left running could write to memory. Strict closes it: its kernel's built-in command line carries efi=disable_early_pci_dma, and its kernel check requires it. The IOMMU is on from boot in strict mode.

Thunderbolt is left out. A Thunderbolt or USB4 port is a PCIe slot on the outside of the case; without the Thunderbolt driver it carries USB and DisplayPort and nothing that speaks PCIe. The computer's own SATA, NVMe and eMMC disks are invisible: their drivers are not built.

Raspberry Pi 5 Later

The Pi 5 comes after the Pi 4.

What every build leaves out

Strict and Compatible, on the PC and the Pi. Each kernel is built with a fixed list of drivers.

  • No network stack. No Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or USB network driver, and no socket layer for one to use. A Pi's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip is never given its firmware.
  • No kernel modules. The drivers are built in, and kernel lockdown is forced to its strictest level from early boot.
  • No desktop. No X11, Wayland, window manager, GPU driver, toolkit or browser engine: one Rust program lays out every screen and writes its pixels.
  • No login. No getty, su, sudo or passwd, and root's password is locked. Every process that handles input runs as an unprivileged user.
  • Nothing written to the computer. No driver for its disk and no access to its firmware variables. The system runs from memory, and nothing is mounted from the boot medium after it starts.
  • No swap, suspend, hibernation or core dumps. Nothing in memory is paged out.
  • No logs. The release images write no boot report and no timings, and their kernel log stays closed to the app.
  • No new keyboard without the code. A keyboard other than the built-in one types nothing until it types a random code shown on the screen (below).

Cameras

A USB webcam that offers uncompressed frames (YUYV) works, plugged in or built in. A webcam that sends only MJPEG frames does not work yet.

On a Pi, a USB camera is the camera for every screen. For a housing, a bare USB camera board with screw holes and an adjustable (M12) lens fits well: screw it in place and set its focus once at the distance a QR code is held.

The 2.8-inch build also takes a Raspberry Pi Camera Module v1 or v2 on the board's camera connector. The HDMI and DSI builds do not. Camera Module 3 is not supported.

Not working yet: a MIPI camera. Most laptop webcams made after about 2020 are sensors on a MIPI lane behind an image processor, and Faraday carries neither their driver nor its firmware. Such a computer shows the scanner's file path and no viewfinder; a USB webcam in a port works. A webcam plugged in after the system starts is not picked up.

Touchscreens

On a PC: a built-in touchscreen, and a USB one once approved.

On the Pi's Strict build the panel's own touch is the only input. The Compatible build adds USB touchscreens; the one the screen's profile names, such as the 10-inch HDMI panel's, is trusted input and needs no approval.

Keyboards and pointers

A keyboard other than the computer's built-in one is ignored until it types a random code shown on the screen, and until it is unplugged if it types three codes' worth of letters without it. After three such keyboards, new ones are ignored until Faraday starts again.

A new pointer is ignored until a person approves it with trusted input. USB interfaces are authorised one at a time: a stick that also claims to be a keyboard types nothing.

Files

FAT only, read and written by Faraday's own disk process in Rust, not by the kernel. A partition holding anything else is left alone.

On a PC: USB sticks, and an SD card in a USB reader. On the Pi's Strict build: its own card slot; the Compatible build adds USB storage.

Help test

An image is published before it has been tried on its hardware, marked Looking for testers. Once it has been tried there, its screen's settings are confirmed and its page marks it Tested. The pages never say whose hardware it was tried on.

How to report

Open an issue on github.com/bitcoinpasada/faraday with the make and model of the computer or board, the screen, the image's file name, and what happened.

Where it does not start, When the stick does not start goes first.

The dev image

Built from the source with dev=1, it shows the kernel's messages on the screen and writes faraday-boot-report.txt every 30 seconds: the kernel log, the screen and devices found, and no secret. Send that file, and the build ID (uname -r on its console), with the report. Never use the dev image for keys that matter.

Considered, not supported

Boards studied and Macs planned. Faraday does not run on them yet.

Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6-Touch-LCD-7B Under study

A 7-inch 1024 × 600 IPS board with GT911 capacitive touch, MIPI-DSI and MIPI-CSI. It has an ESP32-P4 (two RISC-V cores, up to 400 MHz, 768 KB SRAM, 32 MB PSRAM, 32 MB flash) and an ESP32-C6 module for Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5. It costs about US $50. Studied without a board on 2026-10-28. Not supported, and not being ported.

What stands in the way

  1. Vault memory. Faraday makes vaults of at least 64 MiB of Argon2 memory, and the board has 32 MB in all. A vault it could open would be made at 16 MiB or less, below Light. That is the owner's decision, not taken.

  2. The radio. The C6 boots its own flash at power-on, whatever the P4 runs. Without a radio means the C6 module desoldered, or its flash erased and the erase checked. No ESP32-P4 board without a C6 was found. ESP32-S3 boards carry the radio in the chip and are not candidates.

  3. The security model. docs/SECURITY-MODEL.md rests on Linux:

    • separate users and processes
    • the disk process alone touching sticks
    • a fresh process at Lock in pages the kernel zeroes
    • lockdown and no modules

    One ESP-IDF image in one address space would need a model of its own.

  4. Secure boot. Espressif's advisory AR2026-006 says the P4's ROM ECDSA check may accept invalid signatures, so RSA-3072 secure boot would be used. ESP-IDF is a large body of C that Faraday has not reviewed.

What would decide it

Help is welcome. Before any port, these would be measured on the board:

  • A full frame and a scroll step drawn by faraday-draw
  • Argon2id at 16 MiB
  • A 640 × 480 QR frame read by rqrr
  • A PSBT signed
  • A check, with a scanner, that an erased C6 stays silent

Macs Coming

Planned, not built. Intel Macs will start the PC stick, with one added keyboard driver. Apple Silicon Macs, M1 and M2 first, will start a stick image of their own, through a start-up file Faraday Starter installs once on the Mac and Apple's own recovery system seals. The M3 comes later; the M4 and M5 cannot start it yet.

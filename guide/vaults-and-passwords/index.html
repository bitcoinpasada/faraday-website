    Vaults, passwords and codes

    A vault is one file, .fvlt, sealed under one to four passphrases. Faraday opens it in memory, seals it again at every lock, and writes it to a stick on the next stick visit. The passphrases on this page are examples only: never use a passphrase printed anywhere, this page included.

    You end with
    a vault of your own on a stick, holding key 3dca6805, a login with its TOTP codes and a note; the vault written, locked and unlocked again, and the login exported for KeePass.
    Before you start
    Make a key. Back it up makes a vault inside a backup's checklist (step 4.8); this page makes one on its own, for keys and for everything else.
    You'll need
    • Faraday with key 3dca6805 loaded and no stick attached.
    • A USB stick other than the Faraday stick, formatted FAT32.
    • For a passphrase from dice: five dice, or one die rolled five times.
    • For the codes: a site's TOTP setup code (its QR code or its key), and a phone or watch showing the time to the minute.
    Time
    about 30 minutes.

    1Create a vault

    On Home, press Create a vault (None yet), or press Vaults in the sidebar, then Create a vault at the top right. With a stick in, the page says Remove the stick, then type the passphrases: pull it first.

    Create a vault is a column of cards. Size is closed on PCs and a Raspberry Pi · 64 MiB · 3 passes, with Change:

    SizeUnlock memoryOpens on
    PCs and a Raspberry Pi64 MiB, 3 passesany PC and a Raspberry Pi
    PCs only512 MiB, 3 passesPCs only

    Each row gives its unlock time on this computer. Customise opens three cards in place of Size:

    CardChoices
    Where will you open it?This computer and each other kind of machine, Other PCs and a Raspberry Pi 3 among them, all ticked as Size had them; the cost suggested next fits the one with the least memory
    Unlock costLight, Standard, Medium, Strong, Maximum and Custom, each with its memory, passes and the bits it adds; one reads Suggested, the others Fits or "Too large for" the weakest machine
    Space per passphrasethe room each passphrase's contents get, and the file's size

    The cost and the space are fixed for the life of the vault. Leave Size as it is.

    Under Name and passphrases:

    1. Name: type Passwords. Beside it, File Passwords.fvlt. The name is what anyone who sees the stick reads; leave it empty for vault.fvlt.
    2. Passphrase 1: type it in the first field, then again in the second, until the line under them reads Matches. The eye at the end of a field, or Show passphrases, shows what is typed. Or press Generate with dice (step 2).
    3. Add another passphrase adds Passphrase 2, up to four, each with Remove. Each passphrase opens contents of its own, and the file does not say how many there are. For this walkthrough, keep one.

    Steps only and Guided at the top switch a short walk-through on each card off and on. The panel on the right, Your choices, repeats each card's choice; under Will create it lists the File, its Size, the Unlock memory, the time to unlock here (Unlock here), what the cost adds (Unlock cost adds), the passphrase's Strength, and where it goes (Goes to: For the stick). Press Create vault.

    "Creating" and the time it takes show while it works. A sealed copy of the vault goes into Files, For the stick.

    2A passphrase from dice

    Generate with dice on a passphrase's card opens the dice:

    On the cardWhat it is
    Which list?Long list (7776 words · five dice), Short list (1296 words · four dice), Short list 2 (1296 words · 3 edits apart)
    "Roll 5 dice for each word and type the numbers"the rolls, typed as they land in the field at the foot, or pressed on its keys 1 to 6; each word appears as its dice are in, and a press on a word shows it in the list
    the strength lineunder the field: the words so far, their bits, and the bits with the unlock cost
    Use these wordsputs the words in both fields
    Cancelleaves the field as it was

    The card asks for six words or more of the long list; the count turns green at 77 bits. Six words of the long list are 77.5 bits. Once the words fill the field, the strength line reads the dice's bits plus the cost's. A typed passphrase's own bits are not measured, and the line says so.

    In Practice (Faraday Demo), Practice puts faraday in place of these words: the vault's passphrase is faraday, the public one every Practice vault uses.

    3Unlock it

    Once made, the vault waits on Unlock, picked, as New vault, Locked · not written yet, with Type the passphrase once more to open it. Type the passphrase under Passphrase and press Unlock. Under the button, "This computer:" and how much memory it has free; the unlock cost is not shown here.

    "Unlocking" and its time show while it works. A wrong passphrase leaves the page as it is, with That passphrase opens nothing in this vault under the field; there is no limit on tries.

    The vault opens on its contents: Nothing in it yet, Sealed once under its name, and a button per thing it can hold:

    ButtonAdds
    Save a bitcoin keya key loaded now (step 4)
    Save a walleta wallet loaded now
    Add an entrya login: title, username, password, URL, notes, TOTP secret (step 5)
    Add a notetext (step 6)
    Make a GPG keyA GPG key
    Make Secure Boot keysYour own Secure Boot keys
    Write it to a stickwrites the vault as it is now (step 9)

    4The contents, and a key saved

    Press Save a bitcoin key. Save a key lists each key loaded and not in the vault: 3dca6805 and its name, with Save. A key loaded with a BIP-39 passphrase also has Save with its passphrase. Press Save. The line Every key in this session is in the vault shows once all are in.

    The page is now the vault's contents, in three columns:

    PartWhat it shows
    the titlethe vault's name, then Open, or "Open ·" and the unsaved changes (Open · 1 unsaved change); Rename and Lock on the right; under it the file's name and its cost (Passwords.fvlt · 64 MiB · 3 passes)
    the first columneach kind the vault holds, with its count: Bitcoin keys, Wallets, Entries, Notes, GPG keys, Secure Boot; then a row per kind to add, each with +: Bitcoin key, Wallet, Entry, Note, GPG key, Secure Boot
    the second columnthe items of the kind chosen, with its add button (Save a key, Save a wallet, Add an entry, Add a note)
    the third columnthe item chosen

    Key 3dca6805 shows its length and fingerprint, its Fingerprint, its Words and its SeedQR (Show SeedQR) behind their eyes, BIP-39 passphrase (Stored or Not stored), Load at unlock (Yes or No) and In this session (Loaded or Not loaded). Under them:

    ButtonDoes
    Load into sessionloads the key, when it is not loaded
    Load with a passphraseloads it with a BIP-39 passphrase typed now
    Don't load at unlock, Load at unlockwhether the unlock's load list ticks it (step 8)
    Save to a stickthe key's words or SeedQR as a file for the stick, outside the vault: Anyone who finds this stick has this seed
    Hold to delete from vaultremoves the key from the vault

    A key in a wallet of the same vault reads Load at unlock "Loads with" and the wallet's name. A wallet's item shows its backup's chart, its Descriptor, Open in Wallets, Show as QR and Save to a stick.

    A value behind an eye shows after a press on it and hides at the next press or when the page is left. Rename names the vault's contents; the file keeps its name.

    5A login with its TOTP code

    Press Entry, the row with + under the kinds (or Add an entry once Entries is listed). Fill in, as an example:

    FieldExample
    TitleMail
    Usernameme@example.com
    Passwordthe site's password
    URLthe site's address
    Notesanything else
    TOTP secretthe site's setup key, or the otpauth:// link its QR code holds

    Press Save, or Cancel. An entry needs a title. A TOTP secret that does not read is refused with Not a TOTP key, and the form keeps what is typed.

    Under Entries, beside Add an entry, are Scan a code and TOTP codes (step 6); two buttons fill the list:

    ButtonDoes
    Scan a codeopens the camera; a site's TOTP setup QR code fills a new entry's form with its title, account and secret, to save
    "Import from Files" and a countadds every entry of the entries files in Files

    Entries that do not fit the vault's space are not added, and a line says how many bytes they take and how many are left. With entries in the list, Search entries over it keeps those whose title holds what is typed; usernames, URLs and notes are not searched.

    The entry shows its title and kinds ("Password · TOTP"), Username, Password, URL, Code, TOTP secret and Notes, the secrets behind their eyes, then Edit, Export for KeePass (step 7) and Hold to delete from vault. Code is the code now, with the seconds left; it reads Set the time while the clock is not checked (step 6).

    6The codes, and the time

    Press TOTP codes under Entries, or Tools → TOTP codes (Codes from the entries in open vaults). The first time after power-on, Set the time opens first:

    On Set the timeWhat it is
    Nowthe clock as Faraday reads it, or Not known
    Time zonethe zone, with - and +
    Date, Timetoday's date and the next minute, typed
    Setsets the clock to that minute at its first second
    Keep this timekeeps the clock as it is, where it is right
    Backgoes back without checking the clock

    Set is grey until a date and a time are typed. Type the next minute, and press Set when the phone's minute changes to it. The codes open:

    RowWhat it shows
    "Now · UTC" and the zonethe local time with seconds, and Set the time
    an entryits title and account, its code, and a bar for the seconds left
    an entry whose secret is a counter's (HOTP)a badge, no code
    an entry whose secret does not readUnreadable

    A row opens its entry. Search codes finds an entry by its title. Under the list, Scan a setup code and Add an entry add to the vault open; with no entry, No entries with a TOTP secret. The codes come from every vault open.

    7Export a login for KeePass

    On the entry Mail, press Export for KeePass. Type a passphrase of 8 characters or more under Passphrase (as an example, correct horse battery staple), then Seal and export; Cancel leaves it.

    Sealing shows while it works. The file goes into Files, For the stick, named from the entry's title in lower case, mail.kdbx, and a line says "mail.kdbx waits for the stick, sealed". It holds that one entry and opens in a KeePass app with that passphrase. KeePass export under Tools says the same: an entry leaves this way, one at a time.

    8Lock

    Press Lock in the vault's title (or on its row in Vaults, or at the foot of the sidebar). With changes in the vault, the Lock sheet lists what it keeps and wipes (Lock):

    SectionLists
    Keptthe vault, and the files for the stick
    Wiped from memorykey 3dca6805, "in" the vault; a key or wallet in no vault reads "nowhere else", with Save beside it to put it in the vault open
    ThenFaraday restarts on Unlock

    Press Lock, or Back to the vault. The lock seals the vault with its changes for the stick, wipes every key, and starts Faraday again on Unlock.

    9Write it to a stick

    Plug the stick in. With nothing loaded, Stick visit opens: under In Faraday, Passwords.fvlt and mail.kdbx are ticked. Press Write 2 files. Each file is written, read back and compared; the visit's line reads "Wrote Passwords.fvlt, read back and matched". Then pull the stick. Files, sticks and QR codes goes through the visit.

    Vaults lists each vault Faraday holds; the one picked shows its file, then where it stands, what it holds and when it was seen ("On KEYSTICK · current · key 3dca6805 · 2 entries · seen" and the time), and Unlock. Where it stands comes first:

    LineMeaning
    Never writtensealed for the stick, on no stick yet
    "On" a stick's name "· current"the stick holds it as it is now
    Changed since writtenchanged since the stick was written
    Unchangedcopied in from a stick Faraday no longer knows, and not changed

    A changed vault makes Home lead with "Write Passwords to a stick". A vault on the stick already is written over its own copy; another file of its name is kept, and this one is written beside it.

    10Unlock it again, and load from it

    After a power-off the vault is only on its stick. Plug the stick in with no key loaded: on Stick visit, Passwords.fvlt is ticked under On the stick. Press Import 1 file, then pull the stick. A vault on the Faraday stick itself comes in at start instead, on Read from … (Read from the boot stick).

    Then press Unlock on the vault's row in Files or in Vaults, type the passphrase and press Unlock. Files opens, with Load from Passwords over it (the vault's name; "Load from Vault" for one without a name), and a count of what it holds ("1 key"):

    On the sheetWhat it is
    a card per walletthe wallet and its seeds this vault holds, ticked as its Load at unlock says
    Keyskeys in no wallet: 3dca6805, ticked
    Loadedgreyed at the right of a line already loaded
    Unselect all, Select allunticks every card while any is ticked; ticks every card once none is
    Load tickedloads what is ticked; Wallets opens on a wallet loaded, otherwise Files
    Not nowloads nothing; the vault stays open

    Press Load ticked. A flow that asks for a vault (Spend, Restore, a backup) unlocks it from its own step and loads what it needs, with no sheet.

    The vault's page now reads "Sealed 2 times · last", the date and the time: the lock in step 8 sealed it once more. Had Files held a copy sealed more times, the unlock would have stopped at Open this copy?, saying "An older copy: sealed 2 times." and the count of the newer one, and opened only on Open.

    11A new passphrase

    Passphrases, the cost and the space are fixed when a vault is made. To change one, create a new vault, unlock both, and save into the new one what the old one holds: load the keys and wallets and Save them, and add the entries and notes again. Write the new vault, then delete the old file from its stick with zeros (Files, sticks and QR codes): until it is deleted, the old file still opens with the old passphrase.

    With your own key

    • Roll the passphrase with real dice: six words of the long list. Never use a passphrase printed anywhere, or one used for anything else.
    • Write the passphrase down and keep it apart from the stick: a lost passphrase loses what it opens.
    • Name the vault for whoever may see the stick, or leave the name empty.
    • Before you rely on the vault, unlock it from its stick on a fresh start: Before you rely on a key.
    • A KeePass export is as safe as its passphrase: give it a strong one of its own.
